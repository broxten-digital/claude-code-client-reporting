# Client context: Brightside Dental Group

> This file is the product. The prompt is not.
>
> Everything below is what a platform export cannot tell you and what the model
> has to be told before its analysis is worth reading. Written once per client,
> paid out every month after that.
>
> **All data in this project is invented. Brightside Dental Group is not a real
> business.**

## The business

Three-location general and cosmetic dental group in a single metro. Northside
and Lakeview are established practices. Westgate opened 14 months ago and is
still filling its schedule, so it is the growth location and it is allowed to
run less efficiently than the other two.

Revenue is driven by booked appointments, not by traffic. The website exists to
produce booked appointments and nothing else.

## What a conversion is, and what one is worth

A conversion is a **booked appointment request**, either a form submission or a
tracked call. It is not revenue. It is not a closed case.

| Service | Booked value in the ad platform | Close rate | Real expected value |
|---|---|---|---|
| New patient exam and cleaning | $480 | 80% | $384 |
| Emergency visit | $650 | 72% | $468 |
| Implant consult | $3,800 | 35% | $1,330 |

**The platforms report the middle column.** Conversion value in the Google Ads
export is booked value at full case price, so it overstates expected revenue by
roughly 3x on implants and by a little on everything else. Any revenue or ROAS
figure in a client report has to apply the close rate first.

## What the quarter is for

1. **Fill Westgate.** It is the only location with open chair time. Volume at
   Westgate is worth more than efficiency at Westgate.
2. **Grow the implant line.** Launched August 1 at Westgate only. Implants are
   the highest-value service and the practice wants a repeatable acquisition
   channel for them. A higher cost per implant consult is an accepted cost.
3. **Protect the cleaning base at Northside and Lakeview.** These two are near
   capacity. The goal is holding cost per booking flat, not growing volume.

## What the client already knows and does not need explained

- What CPC, CTR and impression share mean. Do not define terms.
- That August is slow. Families are dealing with school, not teeth.
- That the phones are answered by a front desk, not a call center, and that
  after-hours calls go to voicemail.

## What the client actually asks in the meeting

Every month, in this order:

1. Did we book more patients than last month, and at what cost?
2. Is Westgate filling up?
3. Is the implant thing working yet?
4. What are you changing next month?

A report that does not answer those four is not finished, whatever else is in
it.

## Decisions this report can influence

Budget shifts between locations, budget shifts between services, pausing or
adding campaigns, and website or landing page changes. It cannot influence
pricing, staffing, hours, or insurance mix, so recommendations in those areas
are wasted words.

## Reporting rules

- Compare to the same month last year where the data exists, then to last
  month. Month over month alone is misleading in a seasonal business.
- Segment by location before segmenting by anything else. Blended numbers hide
  the only story anyone at this client cares about.
- Never report a metric moving without saying whether that movement was good.
  "Cost per conversion rose 22%" is not a finding. It is a fact waiting for one.
- Every claim traces to a number in the export. If it cannot be traced, it does
  not ship.
