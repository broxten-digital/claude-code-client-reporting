# Caveats and known distortions: Brightside Dental Group

> Every caveat here is a wrong conclusion the system will never reach again.
>
> Add to this file every time the analysis gets something wrong. That is the
> whole maintenance routine. It should grow for about three months and then go
> quiet.

## Known events that break comparisons

**July 14 to July 22, 2026: form conversions under-recorded.**
The form vendor was swapped and the conversion tag did not fire for nine days.
Roughly 70% of conversions come from forms and about a third of those still
recorded, so the window shows around 45% of true conversions. Traffic was
unaffected.

- **Never** compare July totals to another month without saying this.
- July's true conversion run rate is the 22 clean days annualized to the month,
  not the reported total.
- The fingerprint to recognize: sessions, clicks and impressions all held while
  conversions fell off a cliff. A real demand drop moves traffic too. If
  conversions move alone, suspect tracking before you suspect performance.

**July 1 to July 31, 2026: $99 new patient special.**
Ran on non-brand cleaning campaigns only. It lifted conversion volume and cut
cost per booking for that month. August is not worse than July. August is
normal and July was promoted. Say so before anyone panics.

**August 1, 2026: implant campaign launched at Westgate.**
New service line, new campaign, first month. Any blended August number that
includes it is comparing a mixed account to a single-service account. Report
implants separately for at least three months, and report the rest of the
account on its own so the base is still legible.

**Mid-August onward: back to school.**
Elective and hygiene demand softens roughly 15 to 20% in the last two weeks of
August. Emergency demand does not move, because emergencies do not care about
the school calendar. A softening that shows up in cleanings but not in
emergency is seasonal, not a performance problem.

## Structural distortions that are always true here

**Brand campaigns flatter every blended number.** Brand converts at four to
five times the rate of non-brand and costs a fraction as much per click.
Reporting one blended cost per conversion for the account makes performance
look better than it is and hides non-brand entirely. Always split branded from
non-branded before quoting an efficiency number.

**Conversion value is booked value, not revenue.** See the close rates in
`context.md`. Apply them before any revenue or ROAS claim reaches the client.

**Weekends are structurally low** for everything except emergency. Do not read
a Saturday as underperformance.

**Emergency traffic converts differently.** Emergency searchers call more than
they fill out forms, and call tracking undercounts them. Do not hold the
emergency page to the same conversion rate as a location page, and do not size
an opportunity by assuming it could reach one.

## Rules for how to reason

- **Do not size an opportunity by applying the best-performing segment's rate
  to the worst-performing segment.** Different intent converts differently. If
  you quote an upside number, say what rate you assumed and why it is
  reachable.
- **A metric moving is not a finding.** State what changed, why it changed, and
  whether that was the intended outcome.
- **Say when you do not know.** A stated unknown is more useful than a
  confident guess, and the guess is what destroys trust when the client checks.
- **Nine days of data is not a trend.** Neither is one week.
